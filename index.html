Speaker playbook added

Distress, off-market leads, county expansion, manufactured housing

Do first

Watch foreclosure filings weekly

Speaker claim: Jefferson County foreclosure filings can sit roughly 12 months before auction. That gives time to identify owners, condition, equity, liens, and possible exits before the crowd sees an auction date.

  • Source to add: jeffcomm.org foreclosure filings.
  • Track filed date, address, owner, loan plaintiff, estimated sale clock, equity, and contact status.
  • Score these as early-distress leads, not normal MLS listings.

Lead gen

Build the people feed

Deals should come from relationships before listings. Add a lightweight CRM lane for wholesalers, REO/BPO agents, probate attorneys, lenders, builders, and county-by-county deal sources.

  • Respond to wholesalers even when passing, so Eric stays on the active-buyer list.
  • Ask agents for drip campaigns by buy box, including Southern Indiana and counties touching Jefferson.
  • Find REO/BPO agents tied to servicers and bank-owned inventory.

Geography

Widen the buy box by one hour

Keep core Louisville neighborhoods, but add Bullitt, Shelby, Spencer, Oldham, Henry, Hardin, Southern Indiana, Frankfort, Campbellsville/Taylor County, and other one-hour markets where buyer competition may be thinner.

  • Flag properties with tall grass, vacancy signs, probate hints, code issues, and stale ownership.
  • Keep crime and tenant-quality filters strict. Cheap is not the same as good. Annoying, but true.

New angle

Manufactured housing / HB 160

Speaker thesis: Kentucky HB 160 effective July 1, 2026 may make manufactured homes harder for local governments to exclude from single-family areas, subject to HUD standards, architecture rules, utilities, foundations, and HOA exceptions.

  • Add vacant lots, large subdividable lots, and infill parcels as deal types.
  • Model lot + home + utility hookup. Speaker placeholder: $44k dealer-cost single-wide, $20k utility/setup, $1,300-$1,400/mo rent, permanent foundation required for normal resale/appraisal path.
  • Verify law, zoning, flood, setbacks, sewer/water tap, and foundation requirements before treating this as real underwriting.

Action plan from the transcript

1. Financing: identify lender/hard-money/private-money options before offers. If the deal is real, money follows faster.

2. Daily/weekly leads: foreclosure filings, probate attorneys, wholesaler lists, agent drips, tall-grass driving routes, and social posts that Eric buys houses cash/quick close.

3. Analyze fast: each lead needs ARV, rehab, cash needed, financing path, rent, exit, safety, and who can help if fear shows up wearing a fake mustache.

4. Be ready to assign: if capital is not ready, a strong lead can still become an assignment/wholesale opportunity instead of a missed deal.
